Ilya Monosov, PhD

Principal Investigator · Bloomberg Distinguished Professor

Ilya E. Monosov, PhD, is a neuroscientist and Bloomberg Distinguished Professor at Johns Hopkins University, with a primary appointment in Neuroscience and secondary appointments in Biomedical Engineering, Electrical and Computer Engineering, and Psychiatry. He directs the Laboratory of Adaptive and Maladaptive Behavior and is a member of the Zanvyl Krieger Mind/Brain Institute and the Data Science and AI Institute.

His research spans biological and machine intelligence, including the mechanisms of decision making under uncertainty, curiosity in animals and machines, emotion and brain-body interactions, and circuit and computational models of learning and memory. He also studies how the circuits supporting these functions break down in disorders of mood and cognition, and how they might be restored, particularly in treatment-resistant conditions.

By uncovering how biological circuits produce adaptive and maladaptive behavior and translating these principles into computational psychiatry and machine learning, he aims to improve mental and physical health and to build algorithms that better serve human goals.

Ethan S. Bromberg-Martin, PhD

Associate Research Scientist

I am Ethan S. Bromberg-Martin, an associate research scientist in the Monosov lab at Johns Hopkins University. I study how the brain learns what things are good and what things are bad, and how it uses this knowledge to banish the bad things and gather the good things! I am especially interested in how our brains motivate us to seek knowledge about the world. In a series of studies along with my collaborators over the years, I have uncovered neural networks in frontal cortex and basal ganglia that guide information seeking. Both in and outside the lab, I believe that learning about the world is not simply a passive process of parameter estimation and error-reduction, but an active delight of exploration and discovery. Why else be a scientist?

Takaya Ogasawara, PhD

Assistant Research Scientist

I am Takaya Ogasawara, an Assistant Research Scientist in the lab. I study the neural mechanisms underlying novelty-seeking behavior. Humans and animals are motivated to explore new objects that they have never encountered before. This tendency persists even when seeking novelty is non-instrumental – i.e., when it does not contribute to reward rates or facilitate new learning. Such non-instrumental novelty seeking is a core component of curiosity that supports adaptive behavior and survival. I investigate neural circuits that regulate this behavior, using electrophysiological recordings and chemogenetic circuit manipulation. Outside the lab, I enjoy leather crafting.

Ruoxi (Kevin) Liu

Undergraduate · Computer Science

I'm a senior undergraduate at Johns Hopkins University, double majoring in Computer Science and Neuroscience. My research interests lie in natural language processing and machine learning, with a focus on interpretable representation learning. I study how directly rewarding specific properties of LLMs' internal representations can encourage desirable model behaviors. I work with Professor Ilya Monosov on quantifying uncertainty in LLMs through probing. Our goal is to use the structure of models' hidden representations as a direct signal for guiding exploration-exploitation decisions toward actions that reduce predicted uncertainty about a question of interest.
